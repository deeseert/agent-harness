# Operating principles

How I decide what to build and when to stop. Change these to yours.

## The experiment rules

1. **Sell before you build.** Every idea reaches someone who can pay within 14 days, with the minimum code.
2. **Maximum two experiments at a time**, 14 to 28 days each, with a small fixed budget each.
3. **The only signal is money**: payments, pre-orders, signed pilots. Likes and compliments don't count.
4. **Decide on the date.** No signal, stop without drama. Strong signal, double down.
5. **Never claim what isn't true**: results, clients, testimonials, reviews, scarcity.

## Ship, then improve

Build the smallest version that works end to end and looks professional, put it in front of real users, and fix what they run into. Perfectionism is the main blocker.

## One piece at a time

Build the core, get it right, then add the next piece. Don't build everything at once.

## Sell the result, not the technology

The customer doesn't buy "a Next.js website". They buy more bookings, more sales or time saved. Talk about what they get (from Alex Hormozi's value equation: a bigger outcome, more certainty it will work, less waiting and less effort for them).

## User stories

Plan features from the user's side: "As a [role], I want [action] so that [benefit]". Every story has a test that says when it's done.

## Document, demonstrate, duplicate

1. **Document** a process while you do it (a playbook in this folder).
2. **Demonstrate** that it works (a case study, a recording, real numbers).
3. **Duplicate** it by handing the playbook to an agent, a person or an automation.

## Getting customers, in order

1. **Warm outreach**: people who already know you.
2. **Cold outreach**: manual first, until a message works.
3. **Free content**: show the work in public.
4. **Paid ads**: only to scale what already works.

Don't jump to step 4 before steps 1 and 2 work (from Alex Hormozi's *$100M Leads*).
