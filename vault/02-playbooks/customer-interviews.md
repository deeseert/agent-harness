# Customer interviews

Use this before choosing what to sell, and before building. The method follows *The Mom Test* (Rob Fitzpatrick) and the Jobs To Be Done switch interview (Bob Moesta).

## Rules

- Ask about what people **did**, not what they **would do**. Past facts count. Opinions and "yes, I'd buy it" don't.
- Ask about the **last time**, with specifics: date, product, brand, shop, price, how long it took.
- Don't pitch during the questions. Mention your idea only at the end.
- Listen more than you talk. Write down their exact words, they're useful later for copy and search.
- Friends and family: their facts count, their enthusiasm doesn't. The real test is strangers paying.

## Questions

**The last purchase**

1. Tell me about the last time you bought [product]. When was it, and what exactly did you buy?
2. Why did you need it then?
3. Where did you look first? Was it in stock?
4. If not, what did you do: wait, order online, go somewhere else, use something else? How long did it take in the end?
5. How much did you pay, including shipping or travel?

**Habits**

6. How often do you buy it? Roughly how much do you spend a month on it?
7. Which shops or sites do you use, and why those? What annoys you about them?
8. What else do you buy regularly with it?

**The problem**

9. The last time something went wrong, what did it cost you?
10. Do people around you have the same problem? Does anyone buy for others?

**Close**

11. Who else should I talk to?

## Good and bad signals

- **Good:** recent, specific stories. Money or time already spent working around the problem. "I went to three shops." Group orders already happening.
- **Bad:** "it would be nice", "I'd definitely buy it", vague answers, no recent purchase.

## After the interviews: the commitment test

Not a question, a real offer, once you know the product and the price: "I'm ordering [product] for anyone who wants it, it costs €X, I'll bring it on [day]. If you want it, pay now." Count how many pay.
